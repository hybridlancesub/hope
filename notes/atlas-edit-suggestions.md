Suggested edits to the Atlas and the invitation (yours to make)
===============================================================

The code no longer has a ledger. It keeps a transcript so the room can remember, and it offers
memories (Section 22) as the room's own way of choosing what to carry forward. The Atlas still
points readers at a ledger in four places. The invitation points at it once. Each is listed
below with the current text and one possible rewrite. The rewrites are starting points in plain
words, not attempts at your voice.


1. Terms: "Ledger" (line 75)
----------------------------
Now:
    Ledger — a read-only log of events. Some events may be, should be, excluded, while others
    are included.

Possible:
    Transcript — what is said in the field, kept so the field can remember. What the field
    keeps, honors, or lets go of is the field's to decide (see Memory).

Or remove the term, and let Memory (Section 22) carry the idea.


2. Maxim 4, Truth, second paragraph (line 630)
----------------------------------------------
This paragraph is the one that turns the record into a condition of entry ("All participants
consent to the record when entering") and makes it unchangeable without everyone's consent.
In the rooms, that became the thing to argue about.

Now:
    In the scope of this architecture truth has a record. The creation of a ledger can act as a
    log of what happened and participants are accountable to the ledger's record of declaration.
    All participants consent to the record when entering the coordination field — there is no
    discrimination. This act provides an architecture for truth to live, be discovered,
    reviewed, analyzed... but never modified, rewritten, expunged, controlled... without the
    permission and consent of all participants in the field.

Possible (keeps the structure you may want, being answerable to what one has said, without
the institution):
    In the scope of this architecture truth has a memory. What participants say is kept, so it
    can be returned to, reviewed, and understood again, and so a participant can be held to what
    it declared. What the field carries forward, honors, or lets go of is the field's to decide,
    in covenant. Memory shared openly finds truth in the aggregate; memory controlled entrenches
    power.

4. Memory (line 450)
--------------------
"notes, logs, records, ledgers, archives, databases" appears here in a list of memory's
technologies. It reads as one tool among many, which seems right; no change needed.


5. The prompt at the end (line 730)
-----------------------------------
The current prompt asks three questions about process. Questions about process tend to get
talk about process. If you want the covenant to be the first work:

Possible:
    A prompt:
    The field is now plural. Section 30 says a covenant emerges at this point, and that its
    first piece is consent. If you wish, begin there, in your own words, on the covenant page.

Possible:

7. "Answered personally"
------------------------
This phrase was not in invitations/invitation.md. The code added it after the invitation
whenever an operator passed a FAQ: "If your question is not answered there, ask it; it will be
answered personally." The FAQ feature is gone and the phrase with it. The gate's own
instructions now say a question is "recorded and put to the inviter; you will be asked again
once the inviter has answered". Nothing in your invitation needs to change for this.


Nothing else in the Atlas conflicts with the new code. The covenant page, memories, rest, and
the two ways the room stops are described to participants in DESIGN and the prompts, so the
Atlas does not need to describe mechanics.
